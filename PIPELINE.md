# Pipeline, stage by stage

This document explains what the code does and, where a choice was made, why. It is
written to be read alongside the Methods section.

---

## The question the pipeline is built to answer

Murmur energy occupies roughly 20–500 Hz. So do breath sounds, handling artefact,
patient movement and room noise. Because the interference shares the murmur's
spectral support, a frequency-selective stage removes signal along with noise.

One property separates them. A murmur is generated by flow through the heart and is
locked to the cardiac cycle; most interference is not. Restricting analysis to a
defined phase of the cycle suppresses whatever fails to recur at that phase, without
touching the murmur's spectrum.

The cycle reference itself must come from somewhere. Auscultation gives a single
acoustic channel and no electrode, so the pipeline reconstructs a synthetic ECG from
the phonocardiogram and reads the cycle from that reconstruction. Every design
decision below follows from this.

---

## Stage 1: Indexing (`src/data.py`)

CirCor DigiScope distributes one `.wav` phonocardiogram per auscultation site plus a
metadata table with the murmur labels per patient. `build_record_index` pairs the
two. The patient identifier is the leading numeric field of the filename and is
carried through every later stage, because it is what the cross-validation groups
on. The pipeline reads no annotation other than the class labels.

## Stage 2: Synthetic-ECG reconstruction (`src/generator.py`)

A conditional adversarial network, trained on EPHNOGRAM where the ECG and the
phonocardiogram were recorded simultaneously, maps each 8 s phonocardiogram window
at 8 kHz to a synthetic ECG of the same length. Longer recordings are processed in
consecutive windows and the reconstructions are concatenated. Training code and
reconstruction quality are reported in the companion reconstruction paper; this
package consumes the released checkpoint (`weights/README.md`).

The architecture in `src/generator.py` matches the checkpoint exactly and loads with
`strict=True`: a mismatched checkpoint raises instead of silently producing a
partial model.

## Stage 3: Cardiac-phase gating (`src/gating.py`)

R-peaks are detected on the synthetic ECG: bandpass 5–30 Hz in second-order
sections, squared envelope, peak-picking at descending prominence thresholds, then
realignment to the raw-trace maxima and two physiological post-filters (amplitude
consistency and RR-interval regularity). Consecutive R-peaks bound one cardiac
cycle, and fixed fractions of the RR interval divide it into S1 (0–15%), systole
(15–50%), S2 (50–60%) and diastole (60–100%).

Two boundary sources are implemented, and they are the study's independent variable:

| `--boundaries` | what it does |
|---|---|
| `synthetic` | cardiac phases from R-peaks of the reconstructed ECG |
| `fixed` | equal-duration windows at 75 bpm, ignoring the cardiac cycle |

`fixed` is the control, matching the article's equal-time ablation: pseudo-cycles
at a fixed 75 bpm, each split into four equal windows. It keeps the audio, the
features, the classifier and the folds identical and removes the cardiac anchor
from the boundaries (its equal quarters also discard the S1/systole/S2/diastole
proportions), so the contrast is between cardiac-anchored and cardiac-blind
boundaries.

When the detector cannot find at least three plausible peaks it falls back to
envelope peak-picking, and as a last resort to evenly spaced beats at 80 bpm, so
every recording produces a usable peak train. The printed cycles-per-recording
statistics expose how often the peak count is implausible for the duration.

**The detector is modular on purpose.** It consumes the synthetic trace and returns
an array of peak indices; the phase boundaries consume nothing but that array. The
timing precision of these peaks bounds everything downstream, so a stronger
detector, or a segmentation model fused with acoustic S1/S2 evidence, can be swapped
in behind the same interface and should translate into better phase alignment. We
regard this substitution as the most direct route to improving on the results and
invite it.

Recordings with fewer than two complete cycles, or under one second of audio, are
excluded: a single cycle cannot support the cycle-to-cycle variability features.

## Stage 4: Features (`src/features.py`)

464 features per recording, computed at 4 kHz. Each phase-dependent feature is
computed **per cardiac cycle**, then summarised across the recording by its median
and standard deviation. The median is used rather than the mean because a handful of
noisy cycles should not move a recording-level descriptor.

| family | what it captures |
|---|---|
| phase-specific MFCCs | timbre of S1, systole, S2 and diastole separately |
| diastolic descriptors | spectral centroid, bandwidth, rolloff, zero-crossing rate in diastole |
| absolute and relative amplitude | loudness of each phase, and each phase relative to the heart sounds |
| phase transitions | continuity across the S1 to systole and systole to S2 boundaries |
| systolic envelope | shape descriptors (monotonicity, convexity, time to peak, skew, kurtosis) and timing descriptors (occupation ratio, holosystolic score) |
| sub-phase energy | energy distribution within systole, which is what separates early from mid from late |
| cross-phase ratios | murmur energy against heart-sound energy |
| wavelet band energies | multi-resolution energy in the murmur band |
| cycle variability | consistency of the above across cycles |
| recording-level | whole-waveform MFCCs and spectral statistics |

The last family never sees a cardiac boundary. It is identical across both arms by
construction and acts as an internal check that the arms differ only in
segmentation.

The envelope and sub-phase families are the ones that make characterisation possible
at all: grade depends on relative intensity, timing on *where within systole* the
energy sits, shape on the profile of the systolic envelope. None of these can be
computed without knowing where systole begins and ends, which is why the boundary
source is the variable under test.

## Stage 5: Classification (`src/model.py`, `src/evaluate.py`)

Two classifiers are available, matching the article: a multilayer perceptron
(default) and a random forest with 300 trees and balanced class weights
(`--classifier rf`). One MLP architecture covers all four tasks; only the output
width changes.

**Two hidden layers, 128 and 64 units.** Deliberately small. With roughly 200–300
murmur-positive recordings per characterisation task, a larger network memorises the
training fold. Capacity is matched to the label count, not the feature count.

**Focal loss with class weighting.** The characterisation labels are strongly
imbalanced. Without weighting the majority class dominates the gradient and the
minority classes are never learned.

**Early stopping on a patient-disjoint split drawn inside the training fold.** No
participant informs both the stopping decision and the test score.

### The two choices that determine whether the numbers mean anything

**The default fold protocol is the article's: recording-level stratified
ten-fold cross-validation.** A participant contributes up to four recordings, one
per auscultation site, and those recordings are correlated; `--split patient`
groups the folds by participant instead, so no participant's recordings appear on
both sides of a split. Both protocols are reported by the same code paths, and
`results.json` records which one produced the numbers.

**Folds are drawn once per task, from the labels and patient identifiers alone.**
They do not depend on the features, so both arms are scored on identical splits. All
between-arm comparisons are paired, and fold assignment cannot be a source of
difference between them.

**Standardisation is fitted inside each training fold**, never on the full matrix.

## Stage 6: Reporting (`src/evaluate.py`)

**Macro-F1 is the primary metric.** Accuracy is misleading under this imbalance: a
majority-class classifier scores roughly 79% accuracy on detection while
contributing nothing clinically. A majority-class baseline is reported for every
task so the reader can see what each score is being compared against.

**Between-arm comparisons report two statistics.** The paired t-test across the
ten folds is the article's test; both arms are scored on identical folds, so the
pairing is exact. A paired bootstrap interval on the pooled out-of-fold
predictions accompanies it, resampling at the unit of the split (recordings under
the recording-level protocol, participants under the grouped one) with 2,000
resamples and the same resample scoring both arms. Per-fold scores are written to
`results.json`, so either statistic can be recomputed.

---

## What the output contains

| file | contents |
|---|---|
| `results.json` | macro-F1, accuracy, bootstrap interval and majority baseline per task and arm; paired differences between arms |
| `out_of_fold_predictions.npz` | pooled out-of-fold predictions, true labels and patient identifiers |

The predictions are released so that any metric or interval can be recomputed
without rerunning the pipeline, and so that a reader can compute a metric we did not
report.

## Running the comparison

```bash
python run_pipeline.py --data     /path/to/training_data \
                       --metadata /path/to/training_data.csv \
                       --weights  weights/generator.pt \
                       --boundaries both \
                       --out results/
```

This runs both arms and prints the paired difference per task: the contribution of
cycle alignment under this boundary source. `--limit 20` runs a small subset first
to verify the installation end to end.

Runtime is dominated by generator inference and feature extraction. On a GPU the
synthetic arm takes roughly 1–2 s per recording; on CPU alone the generator is the
bottleneck and the full dataset takes several hours. The per-recording loop is
independent across recordings and parallelises without changing any result.

## Determinism

`SEED = 42` fixes the fold draw, the inner validation split and the weight
initialisation. Repeated runs on the same machine with the same hardware reproduce
the same numbers. Small differences across GPU architectures are possible in the
last reported digit; the fold assignment and the bootstrap are unaffected
because both derive from the seeded generator and not from the hardware.
